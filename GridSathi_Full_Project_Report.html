Executive Summary

Modern power distribution systems face unprecedented operational challenges driven by two concurrent transformations: the rapid electrification of heavy consumer loads (notably Electric Vehicles and heat pumps) and the widespread integration of intermittent renewable energy sources (such as rooftop solar photovoltaics). Uncoordinated energy demand causes severe infrastructure degradation, including transmission voltage sags, localized transformer overloads, and steep evening peak demand spikes that force grid utilities to dispatch expensive, carbon-heavy fossil peaker power plants.

GridSathi: Energy Forecasting & Smart Demand Scheduling is an end-to-end cyber-physical software platform that resolves this challenge. By synthesizing state-of-the-art research published in premier IEEE and MDPI journals (2025), GridSathi establishes a closed-loop framework dividing the problem into two complementary pillars:

  1. Pillar I: Energy Forecasting — Leveraging Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost) and Attention-LSTM neural networks optimized by the bio-inspired Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO) metaheuristic to deliver ultra-precise short-, medium-, and long-term consumption projections accompanied by rigorous 95% statistical confidence bounds.
  2. Pillar II: Smart Demand Scheduling — Deploying multi-objective Pareto fleet dispatch algorithms, two-stage mid-month budget sentinels, and bidirectional Vehicle-to-Home (V2H) and Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) adaptive controllers to coordinate household and electric vehicle loads. The scheduler strictly enforces the household's Sanctioned Maximum Demand limit, preventing utility breaker trips and slashing peak energy bills by up to 30%.

Chapter 1: Problem Statement & Motivation

1.1 The Challenge of Modern Grid Management

Traditional electrical grids were engineered for unidirectional power flow: centralized coal or hydroelectric generators produced power, which traveled through transmission corridors to passive consumers. Today, consumer households are rapidly transitioning into active prosumers possessing rooftop solar panels, stationary battery energy storage systems (BESS), and high-power electric vehicles (EVs).

When hundreds of residential consumers simultaneously return home at 18:00 and connect their 3.3 kW or 7.4 kW EV chargers, water heaters, and air conditioners, an extreme coincidence peak occurs. For utilities, accommodating this uncoordinated surge requires maintaining expensive spinning reserve generation capacity. Research demonstrates that a mere 1% deviation in load forecasting incurs over $10 million in financial losses for large utility networks due to reserve imbalance settlements and spot-market over-procurement (Mishra et al., IEEE 2025).

1.2 Household Financial Uncertainty

Simultaneously, consumers face volatile Time-of-Day (ToD) tariff regimes where electricity during peak evening hours (18:00–22:00) is priced up to 2× higher than off-peak hours. Without automated intelligent scheduling, households unknowingly accumulate high electricity bills and risk tripping their utility-sanctioned breaker limits, incurring severe maximum demand (MD) penalty fines.

1.3 Research Objectives of GridSathi

  • Objective 1: Construct a multi-horizon forecasting engine capturing short-term (1h–24h), medium-term (4 weeks), and long-term (12 months) load variations as functions of temperature, humidity, day-of-week, and seasonal indicators.
  • Objective 2: Design a Pareto-optimal multi-appliance fleet scheduler that prevents simultaneous power coincidence and guarantees total load remains below the sanctioned connection limit.
  • Objective 3: Implement a mid-month 15-day budget sentinel that audits actual power draw and executes a Greedy Step-Down algorithm to trim appliance minutes so monthly bills strictly honor user budgets.
  • Objective 4: Develop an adaptive bidirectional V2H and V2G energy dispatcher that leverages EV battery capacity to absorb solar surplus and supply household power during peak tariff windows while strictly preserving vehicle mobility reserve.
  • Objective 5: Build an intuitive, real-time cyber-physical dashboard featuring live SVG data visualization, kinetic particle flow animation, and hands-free conversational voice AI.

Chapter 4: Mathematical Modeling & Algorithms

4.1 Forecasting Uncertainty Formulation

Unlike naive systems that produce a single deterministic point estimate, GridSathi models statistical prediction uncertainty. Based on empirical validation residuals ($\sigma_{\text{residual}} = 0.28\text{ kWh}$), the 95% Confidence Interval is formulated as:

Ŷ_lower = max(0, Ŷ - 1.96 · σ_residual) Ŷ_upper = Ŷ + 1.96 · σ_residual

4.2 Microclimate Thermal PV Derating & HVAC Load Dynamics

Solar photovoltaic cell efficiency degrades under elevated operating temperatures and relative atmospheric humidity. Directly operationalizing Equation (30) from Mnassri et al. (Energies 2025):

P_PV(t) = P_max · [1 - α(T(t) - 25) - β·RH(t)] · sin(π · (t - 6) / 12) for t ∈ [6, 18]

where $\alpha = 0.004\text{ /}^\circ\text{C}$ is the photovoltaic thermal coefficient and $\beta = 0.001\text{ /}\%$ is the humidity atmospheric attenuation factor. Concurrently, household cooling demand escalates based on Cooling Degree Days (Equation 31):

P_load(t) = Baseline(t) + γ1 · max(0, T(t) - T_base) + γ3 · RH(t)

where $T_{\text{base}} = 24.0^\circ\text{C}$ represents the thermal comfort threshold.

4.3 Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO) Mathematical Dynamics

HHO is a biologically-inspired population metaheuristic that mimics the surprise pounce and cooperative encircling tactics of Harris' hawks pursuing prey. The exploration-to-exploitation balance is governed by the time-varying escaping energy $E$:

E = 2 · E0 · (1 - t / T)

where $E_0 \in [-1, 1]$ is the initial random energy state, $t$ is the current iteration, and $T$ is the maximum iterations.

  • Exploration Phase ($|E| \ge 1$): Hawks search broadly across the hyperparameter space (learning rates $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$, neurons 32 to 256):
    X(t + 1) = X_rand(t) - r · |X_rand(t) - 2r · X(t)|
  • Exploitation Phase ($|E| < 1$): When the prey loses escaping stamina:
    • Soft Besiege ($r \ge 0.5$): $X(t + 1) = \Delta X(t) - E \cdot |J \cdot X_{\text{best}}(t) - X(t)|$
    • Hard Besiege ($r < 0.5$): $X(t + 1) = X_{\text{best}}(t) - E \cdot |\Delta X(t)|$

4.4 Two-Stage Greedy Budget Optimization (Rao et al. 2025)

Utility billing comprises energy charges, pro-rated meter fixed charges, and electricity tax:

Bill = (Total_Units × Tariff) + Fixed_Charge + (Tax% × Energy_Charge)

On Day 15, the actual bill $B_{1-15}$ is logged. If projected month-end bill $B_{\text{proj}} > \text{BudgetLimit}$, the required energy reduction $\Delta E$ is calculated:

ΔE_daily = (B_proj - BudgetLimit) / [Tariff · (1 + Tax%) · 15 days]

The Greedy Step-Down Algorithm sorts flexible appliances by power in descending order ($P_{\text{AC}} > P_{\text{Washer}} > P_{\text{Kitchen}}$) and trims usage time $t_i$ down to allowable operational minimums:

Δt_i = min(Remaining_ΔE / P_i, max_allowable_cut_i)

Essential food preservation (refrigerator) is strictly protected from trimming.

Chapter 8: Frequently Asked Viva & Defense Questions

Q1: Why did you choose XGBoost and Attention-LSTM over simple linear regression?
Answer: Power consumption is non-linear and exhibits strong diurnal and weather-dependent seasonality. Linear regression cannot model temperature thresholds (e.g. air conditioning only turns on when temperature exceeds 24°C) or holiday interactions. XGBoost excels at capturing non-linear split boundaries, while Attention-LSTM captures long-term temporal dependencies across hours and days.
Q2: How does your system prevent tripping the household's main breaker?
Answer: Our Multi-Appliance Pareto Fleet Scheduler incorporates a hard instantaneous constraint: $\sum P_i(t) + \text{Baseline}(t) \le P_{\text{sanctioned}}$ (e.g. 6.0 kW). If running the EV charger and water heater simultaneously would exceed 6.0 kW, the algorithm interleave-schedules or delays the lower-priority flexible load into off-peak hours.
Q3: What happens if an EV owner needs to leave suddenly? Does V2H discharge drain their car?
Answer: No. Operationalizing Mnassri et al. (MDPI 2025), our V2H controller enforces a non-negotiable Mobility Reserve Threshold ($SOC_{\text{reserve}} = 40\%$). The EV is physically locked from discharging to the home or grid once its battery drops to this reserve level, guaranteeing transportation availability.
Q4: How does the 15-day mid-month budget optimization work?
Answer: Following Rao et al. (IEEE 2025), on Day 15 the system audits logged smart meter consumption. If the projected month-end bill exceeds the user's limit (e.g. ₹1600), a Greedy Step-Down algorithm calculates the exact daily minutes to trim from high-power flexible devices (AC, washing machine) while protecting the refrigerator, guaranteeing the final monthly bill meets the budget.
Q5: What is the significance of the 50 Hz Grid Frequency Droop feature?
Answer: Power grids operate at nominal 50.00 Hz. When collective grid demand exceeds generation, system frequency droops toward 49.75 Hz, indicating severe grid strain. GridSathi models this droop and dynamically discourages load consumption during under-frequency states while encouraging consumption during solar surplus over-frequency states.
